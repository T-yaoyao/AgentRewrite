1. Cost Efficiency

- Overall cost change: The planner's total estimated cost increased from 2,709,567.58 in the original plan to 3,252,613.39 in the rewritten and enhanced plans, an increase of approximately +20.0%. Although the nominal cost figure rose, this is primarily driven by a change in cardinality estimation rather than by a structurally worse plan: the rewritten plan estimates only 38,396 output groups versus 3,124,377 in the original, and the cost model accounts for the additional Partial HashAggregate and Finalize GroupAggregate stages explicitly. The rewritten plan also reaches the final LIMIT with a dramatically smaller intermediate result set, which typically yields better real-world performance than the nominal cost suggests.
- Cost change at the most expensive plan node: In the original plan, the most expensive node is the top-level Sort at cost 2,717,378.52. In the rewritten/enhanced plans, the most expensive node is the top Hash Join at cost 3,251,783.67, an increase of roughly +19.7%. However, the composition of this cost shifted: instead of paying to join ~13.5M filtered lineitem rows and then sorting ~3.1M grouped rows, the rewritten plan concentrates its cost in the parallel pre-aggregation of lineitem (Finalize GroupAggregate side, ~2.69M) and the small orders/customer join (~537K), leaving only a lightweight final sort of ~38K rows.

2. Plan Characteristics

- Scan type transitions: All plans retain Parallel Seq Scan on lineitem, orders, and customer with the same date/segment filters, which is appropriate given the low selectivity of the filters. No scan-type change (e.g., to Index Scan) occurs, so scan efficiency is unchanged across the three plans.
- Join algorithm refinements: The original plan performs a single large Parallel Hash Join joining lineitem directly with the orders/customer subtree, followed by aggregation. The rewritten plan splits this into two phases: first a small Parallel Hash Join between orders and customer (~600K rows), and then a Hash Join between the pre-aggregated lineitem result (~400K rows) and the joined orders stream (~1.44M rows). This "aggregate-then-join" transformation is the core refinement — the join operates on an order of magnitude fewer rows on the lineitem side.
- Elimination of explicit sorting and reduction of intermediate result sets: The original plan must sort ~1.3M rows per worker (Sort cost ~2.19M) to feed the GroupAggregate via Gather Merge, and then the top-level Sort handles ~3.12M grouped rows. The rewritten plan replaces the row-level grouping sort with hash-based aggregation (Partial HashAggregate with 16 planned partitions, requiring no large sort on the lineitem side), reduces the per-worker finalize sort to ~400K rows, and shrinks the final top-level sort from ~3.12M rows to ~38K rows. The Gather Merge feeding the original GroupAggregate is effectively eliminated in favor of a cheaper Gather on the orders side.

3. Resource Utilization

- Memory usage: The original plan is sort-dominated — two large Sort nodes (per-worker sort of ~1.3M rows plus a global sort of ~3.12M grouped rows) plus a parallel hash build for the full lineitem join. The rewritten plan replaces the per-worker big sort with a Partial HashAggregate using 16 planned partitions (which may spill to disk if work_mem is tight, but bounds the in-memory hash table), and its hash builds are much smaller: the lineitem-side hash now holds ~400K aggregated rows instead of serving a join over ~13.5M raw rows. Net effect: peak memory pressure shifts from massive sort buffers to a bounded, partitioned aggregate hash, a favorable trade for large datasets.
- Worker count adjustment: Both plans use 2 parallel workers on the lineitem scan and 2 on the orders/customer join; no change in parallelism degree. The difference is in how parallelism is exploited — the original parallelizes the join over raw rows, while the rewritten plan parallelizes the partial aggregation, which scales better because partial sums are far smaller to redistribute than raw joined rows.
