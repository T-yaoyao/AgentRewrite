The main bottleneck in the original query is the correlated scalar subquery computing `AVG(ctr_total_return)*1.2` per `ctr_state`, evaluated repeatedly for each row of the materialized CTE. Given the data statistics (catalog_returns ~2.1M rows, filtered to 1998 yields roughly ~10k grouped rows), the base aggregation is cheap, but the per-row subplan made the CTE scan cost extremely high. By pre-aggregating the state-level average into `state_return_threshold` (at most ~50 rows) and hash-joining it, the query becomes single-pass over the CTE plus a tiny join.

The filter `ca_state = 'IL'` must remain on the final current-address join (not on the returning-address state), preserving exact semantics. The CTE is materialized once and scanned twice, which is acceptable and cheaper than recomputing the aggregation.

Optional index consideration if execution is still slow: a composite/covering index on `catalog_returns(cr_returned_date_sk, cr_returning_addr_sk, cr_returning_customer_sk) INCLUDE (cr_return_amt_inc_tax)` and `date_dim(d_year)`.
